About

I am an AI systems engineer and researcher building high-performance infrastructure for intelligent systems. My work sits where machine learning meets low-level systems engineering: inference runtimes, compilers, scheduling policies, and the memory and execution machinery that decides whether a model is merely correct or actually fast.

Most recently I spent three and a half years at Akamai Technologies in London, where I architected a next-generation DNS firewall serving 280M+ daily requests and led an LLM-powered analytics platform processing 5B+ network events monthly. Before that I worked on distributed credit-risk calculation systems at JPMorgan Chase. I hold an MSc in Computer Science with Distinction from Coventry University.

I am currently building Ola-RT, an experimental inference runtime and research platform for adaptive, scalable model execution, and I am seeking PhD opportunities in machine learning systems and optimization.

My research interests sit at the intersection of machine learning systems, operations research, optimization, and systems engineering. Concretely, I am interested in how inference systems should decide — how execution policies, scheduling, and memory management can adapt to workload structure rather than being fixed at deployment time.

Active threads include LLM and VLM inference systems; dynamic inference scheduling and execution policies; KV-cache management, batching, and memory optimization; GPU kernels and ML compilers; distributed and adaptive AI systems; and eBPF and kernel-level security.
