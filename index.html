About

I am an AI systems engineer and researcher building high-performance infrastructure for intelligent systems. My work sits where machine learning meets low-level systems engineering: inference runtimes, compilers, scheduling policies, and the memory and execution machinery that decides whether a model is merely correct or actually fast.

Before starting my PhD I spent several years building large-scale security and AI infrastructure in industry, most recently at Akamai Technologies in London.

I recently began my PhD at Nile University of Nigeria. My research asks how generative AI inference systems should decide: modern serving engines make constant choices about admission, batching, scheduling, KV-cache allocation, placement and parallelism, but those decisions are buried inside engine-specific heuristics, making them hard to describe, compare, analyse or optimize independently of their implementations. I am working towards a formal account of the inference execution policy as a first-class object, and towards optimization and learning methods that act on it.

The experimental vehicle for this work is OlaRT, a Rust inference runtime and research platform built on Burn and CubeCL, designed so that execution decisions are explicit, observable, replaceable and measurable rather than hard-coded.

My interests sit at the intersection of machine learning systems, operations research, and systems engineering. The organizing question is:

How should modern AI workloads execute when latency, throughput, memory, modality, hardware, cost, and service-level objectives all matter at once?

Answering it pulls in formal abstraction, constrained optimization, queueing and stochastic control, online learning under non-stationarity, and a great deal of low-level runtime engineering. Adjacent threads include GPU kernels and ML compilers, multimodal and VLM inference, and eBPF and kernel-level systems.

Research & Projects

Covers LLM, VLM and vision workloads, GPU execution, request scheduling, KV-cache management, continuous batching, distributed inference, runtime cost modelling and learned scheduling.
